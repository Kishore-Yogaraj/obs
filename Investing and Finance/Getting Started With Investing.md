The first step to starting out with your investing journey is to choose a **broker**. 

### What is a Broker
A broker is just the company that holds your investment account executes your trades. You can't buy stocks or ETFs directly from the stock exchange yourself. You need an intermediary with access to the market. The broker provides the account, the app/platform and processes your buy/sell orders. They make money through fees, commissions, or interest on uninvested cash

Once you pick a broker you need to open a **TFSA**

### What is a TFSA
A Tax-Free Savings Account is a registered account type the Canadian government created in 2009. Despite the name, it's not really a "savings account". It's a wrapper you can put around investments. The benefits of a TFSA are in the tax treatment:
- You contribute money you've already paid income tax on
- Any gains, dividends, or interests eared inside the account are **never taxed**. Not when they grow and not when you withdraw
- Withdrawal don't count as income and and don't affect government benefits
- Withdrawn amounts get added back to your contribution room the following calendar year

Every year, there's a contribution limit set by the government. For 2026, it's $7000 and unused room carries forward. Since you turned 18, you've been accumulating room each year. So from 18 years of age to now, you have around $28,000 of room available. You can confirm your exact number by logging into **CRA My Account**

In a TFSA all compounding is untouched. Over decades the difference is enormous. As a student your income is low, so an RRSP doesn't give you much tax benefit yet.

**There's a limit because your TFSA is non-taxable and the government would lose money if people could contribute as much as they wanted**
