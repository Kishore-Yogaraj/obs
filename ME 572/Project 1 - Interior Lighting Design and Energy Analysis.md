### Basis of the Project
We are basically acting as a lighting designer for an office. We need to recommend a lighting a system that gives occupants enough light without using more power or costing more than necessary.
#### Why does this matter?
The choice of lighting can affect a building for decades. More light output will generally mean more electricity use and more heat added to the space which can also change heating and cooling loads. Getting the most powerful or cheapest fixture isn't automatically the right choice. This project will help us balance these considerations and justify our decisions just like we would on a real building.

#### What do we need to decide?
The fixture model is already chosen and every fixture has to be identical.
- A **fixture** is basically the entire assembly of a lighting unit including housing, the sockets (mechanism that secures the bulb) the light source (lamp) and the diffuser (the cover that distributes the glow of the light)
We need to decide:
- How may of these fixtures to use
- Which lumen package to use, from 3000 to 9000 lm
	- **Lumen package** is how bright you want the fixture to be which is dependent on the height, size and layout
- The best arrangement in the ceiling grid
#### The constraints
- Enough light over time
	- The room has to maintain at least 400 lux at desk height (2.5 ft)
		- **Lux** is the metric used to measure illuminance, which is the total amount of light that actually lands on a surface (like a desk, floor, or wall)
		- Lumens measure what is given off
		- Lux measure what actually gets hit
		- Rain leaving a cloud is lumens and lux is the puddle that's created
	- LLF accounts for the system losing output as fixtures age and get dirty, so the design starts our brighter than necessary, and dimming can trim that excess
		- **LLF** stands for light loss factor and its a depreciation multiplier (decimal less than 1.0) used during lighting design to anticipate real-world degradation
		- When a lighting system is brand new it operates at 100% capacity and as time passes the light levels drop due to two main categories
			- Non Recoverable Factors
				- **Lamp Lumen Depreciation:** Physical LED chips naturally lose efficiency and dim slightly as they accumulate operating hours
				- **Luminaire Ambient Temperature:** Overheating can permanently reduce and LED's maximum potential output
			- Recoverable Factors
				- **Luminaire Dirt Depreciation:** Dust, film, and airborne particulates settle on the lenses, diffusers and reflectors which traps light inside the fixture
				- **Room Surface Dirt Depreciation:** Dirt builds up on walls and ceiling which reduces their ability to bounce and reflect ambient light down to the desk level
- Limited power
	- Lighting can't exceed 0.8W/ft^2 or 1408 W in this room
- Even light
	- Fixtures should not be more than about 10 ft apart which prevents us from using only a few very bright fixtures

- The initial total light output in lumens produced by a single light fixture
CU: Coefficient of Utilization
- An efficiency decimal that calculates how much light reaches the desk after bouncing off room surfaces based on room geometry

- Maintenance multiplayer that accounts for factors that will cause the light to naturally dim over time
