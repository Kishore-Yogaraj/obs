It's been an alright summer so far. There's a lot I have done and a lot I haven't done. These past two weeks I've been really distracted with golf for some reason and I don't know how to break out of it. I can look at this as a bad thing or I can look at it as a good thing.

I'm choosing the latter.

Although spending a lot of my time studying and watching video of golf and playing golf has pushed me away from pursuing the things that are important like working on my capstone project, studying for my exam or studying leetcode I think there's a lot to be taken away from this obsession.

**My biggest takeaway:** Obsession is the true driver to enjoying progress and achieving your goals

When your mind is completely consumed by one topic and it's all you can think about your brain does a much better job of allowing you to enjoy the journey and not suffer through the work. I find myself building my own systems and thoughts and connections when I'm driving in the car about golf simply because it's always on my mind. I can't wait to get back home and swing at some balls at the end of everyday. Throughout this process I have developed such a fluid understanding for the golf swing and I'm able to ask the right questions and deduce information a lot better. Plenty of people struggle with getting advice from everywhere on the internet but I've become so obsessed with the sport that I've learned how to build my own perspective on what works and what doesn't work and what advice simply just doesn't make any sense. I'm able to build my own source of truth by piecing together the commonality that people reiterate from video to video. I'm able to search for specific swing fixes that don't directly help my problem but I know will help another problem I have that will domino to my main swing fix. 

An example of this is trying to fix my push slice. A lot of people say if you're slicing the ball then you're coming over the top. This is true but if I spent my whole time worrying about fixing my over the top swing I wouldn't have realized that I had my club face open the entire time. Instead the correct fix was studying the release. In studying the release and practicing my swing I learned about the whip sequence you needed in a golf which led to me to loosen the grip. I developed the whip sequence but I still couldn't release club well. I kept cupping my wrist after and chicken winging my lead arm. I then realized that my lead arm was not rotating the way it should have been. After working on my rotation and release I started toeing the ball a lot more and saw my early extension and started working on that. 

My point is that my obsession with the game led me to develop better judgement over what advice I should and shouldn't listen to. I begin understanding not just what to do but why we do certain movements and began developing my own theories and testing and researching those theories out. None of this felt exhausting to me. It felt very natural and I was always ready to learn more, practice more, and keeping going without getting so frustrated with the process. There were good days and there were bad days. 

This is the kind of obsession you need to bring to the things that are most important in your life and your future. The things that your 25 year old self would want you to be obsessed with. I don't really know if you can manufacture this obsession but you can make an attempt at it. Even if the specific area you are studying doesn't make you obsessed maybe you can find an alternate route to be obsessed about. Something like being obsessed about making your overall life better. When you find enjoyment in the process everything else comes very natural. You're able to reach out to the right people, ask the right questions, research the right topics, find the right resources. It no longer begins to feel like work.

Maybe I'm able to do this a lot better with golf because everyone enjoys golf and everyone who has played has gone through this phase. Either way, I'm going to take this as proof that if I become obsessed enough with an area that I can do anything. It really does come down to putting your mind to it and becoming embraced in the area.

I think one of the more important traits I have to develop is the ability to compartmentalize life. I need to be able to be more in the present and fully embrace the current situation I'm in rather than thinking about what needs to be done or what has been done.
